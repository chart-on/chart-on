# Code of conduct

charton is built by people with different backgrounds, experience levels and first languages. This document says how we treat each other in every project space, and what happens when someone does not.

## Scope

It applies to everyone who takes part: maintainers, contributors, people asking questions and people reporting bugs. It covers the issue tracker, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code review and any other channel the project runs. It also covers conduct elsewhere when someone is speaking for the project, or when the behaviour is aimed at project members because of their involvement.

## What we expect

- **Be respectful.** Disagree with ideas, not with people. Assume the other person means well until they show otherwise.
- **Be patient with newcomers.** Everyone asked a first question once. Point to the docs or an earlier answer without making anyone feel small for asking.
- **Be specific and constructive.** In review, say what should change and why. Explain the trade-off instead of declaring something wrong.
- **Accept decisions gracefully.** Maintainers sometimes say no, or not yet. You can ask for the reasoning and argue your case once, with evidence, in the issue. Then let it rest.
- **Own your mistakes.** Apologise to the people affected and fix what you can.
- **Keep it on topic.** Project spaces are for the project.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, slurs, or demeaning remarks about anyone's identity, background, body, ability, age, religion or beliefs.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Threats, or encouraging anyone to harm themselves or others.
- Publishing someone's private information, such as an address, an email or private messages, without their consent.
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, repeated off-topic posts, pinging people to pressure them, or reopening settled discussions without new information.
- Deliberately misleading others, including impersonating another person or the project.
- Retaliating against someone who reported a problem in good faith.

## Reporting

If someone's behaviour concerns you, tell the maintainers privately. Include links or screenshots and anything else that helps us understand what happened. You do not need to be the person affected to report.

- **Email:** [contact@charton.dev](mailto:contact@charton.dev), the project mailbox.
- **On the platform:** use the "Report content" option in the menu of any comment, issue or discussion. It can go to the repository's maintainers, or to the hosting platform's own moderators for serious abuse.

We read every report, keep the reporter's identity confidential unless they agree otherwise, and reply to acknowledge it within one week. A maintainer who is involved in a report takes no part in handling it.

## What happens next

Maintainers decide on a response in proportion to what happened and to any earlier history. In rising order of severity:

1. **A private note** explaining what was wrong, and sometimes asking for a public correction or apology.
2. **A warning** with a clear statement of what must stop. Further problems lead to the next step.
3. **A temporary ban** from project spaces for a stated period.
4. **A permanent ban.**

Maintainers may also edit or remove comments, commits, issues and other contributions that break this code, and lock threads that have stopped being productive. Serious cases, such as threats or harassment, can go straight to a ban.

If you think a decision about you was wrong, reply to the maintainers privately once with your reasons. They will review it, and the outcome of that review is final.

## Maintainers' responsibilities

Maintainers follow this code too, and are held to it more strictly because they set the tone. They are responsible for explaining it, applying it consistently, and keeping this document up to date.
